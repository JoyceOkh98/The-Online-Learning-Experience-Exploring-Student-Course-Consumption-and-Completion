# The-Online-Learning-Experience-Exploring-Student-Course-Consumption-and-Completion
The Online Learning Course Consumption dataset provides a comprehensive view of the learning behaviour of Freshers, Students, and Working Professionals who consume both Technical and Non-Technical courses across different online learning platforms.
Introduction:

In the EdTech industry, understanding how learners interact with online courses is important for improving course completion, learner satisfaction, and platform growth. The Online Learning Course Consumption dataset provides a comprehensive view of the learning behaviour of Freshers, Students, and Working Professionals who consume both Technical and Non-Technical courses across different online learning platforms.

The dataset contains information such as experience level, course type, learning platform, hours spent per week, course duration, completion status, completion percentage, dropout reason, and satisfaction score. These variables provide an opportunity to understand the factors associated with learners' completion of online courses and their overall learning experience.

This analysis examines how factors such as course type, platform, experience level, hours spent per week, and course duration are associated with learning outcomes. It also explores the reasons learners drop out and how learner satisfaction differs across platforms.

The findings from this analysis can provide useful insights for EdTech platform providers, learners, and course creators. These stakeholders can use the findings to improve course structures, provide better learner support, reduce dropout rates, increase completion rates, and improve learner satisfaction.

Objectives:

1.	To identify course types with the highest and lowest completion rates.
2.	To understand why learners drop out of specific course types.
3.	To identify which course types have the highest completion percentages.
4.	To identify the hours per week associated with higher course completion.
5.	To compare completion rates across different online learning platforms.
6.	To identify platform-specific dropout patterns.
7.	To assess platform performance based on learner satisfaction.
8.	To compare platform effectiveness based on completion percentage.
9.	To understand why learners drop out based on their experience level.
10.	To examine completion rates across different experience levels.
11.	To determine how experience level is associated with completion percentage.
12.	To examine whether course duration is associated with course completion.

Methodology:

The next step taken for this analysis was to input the data into pivot tables in Microsoft Excel to examine the relationship between the independent and dependent variables. The resulting pivot tables were then used to create charts and dashboards that helped to identify patterns in course completion, dropout behaviour, and learner satisfaction.
The analysis focused on Experience Level, Course Type, Platform, Hours Spent Per Week, and Course Duration as the independent variables, while Completion Status, Completion Percentage, Dropout Reason, and Satisfaction Score were treated as the dependent variables.
