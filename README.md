# The-Online-Learning-Experience-Exploring-Student-Course-Consumption-and-Completion
The Online Learning Course Consumption dataset provides a comprehensive view of the learning behaviour of Freshers, Students, and Working Professionals who consume both Technical and Non-Technical courses across different online learning platforms.
Introduction:

In the EdTech industry, understanding how learners interact with online courses is important for improving course completion, learner satisfaction, and platform growth. The Online Learning Course Consumption dataset provides a comprehensive view of the learning behaviour of Freshers, Students, and Working Professionals who consume both Technical and Non-Technical courses across different online learning platforms.

The dataset contains information such as experience level, course type, learning platform, hours spent per week, course duration, completion status, completion percentage, dropout reason, and satisfaction score. These variables provide an opportunity to understand the factors associated with learners' completion of online courses and their overall learning experience.

This analysis examines how factors such as course type, platform, experience level, hours spent per week, and course duration are associated with learning outcomes. It also explores the reasons learners drop out and how learner satisfaction differs across platforms.

The findings from this analysis can provide useful insights for EdTech platform providers, learners, and course creators. These stakeholders can use the findings to improve course structures, provide better learner support, reduce dropout rates, increase completion rates, and improve learner satisfaction.

Objectives:

1.	To identify course types with the highest and lowest completion rates.
2.	To understand why learners drop out of specific course types.
3.	To identify which course types have the highest completion percentages.
4.	To identify the hours per week associated with higher course completion.
5.	To compare completion rates across different online learning platforms.
6.	To identify platform-specific dropout patterns.
7.	To assess platform performance based on learner satisfaction.
8.	To compare platform effectiveness based on completion percentage.
9.	To understand why learners drop out based on their experience level.
10.	To examine completion rates across different experience levels.
11.	To determine how experience level is associated with completion percentage.
12.	To examine whether course duration is associated with course completion.

Methodology:

The next step taken for this analysis was to input the data into pivot tables in Microsoft Excel to examine the relationship between the independent and dependent variables. The resulting pivot tables were then used to create charts and dashboards that helped to identify patterns in course completion, dropout behaviour, and learner satisfaction.
The analysis focused on Experience Level, Course Type, Platform, Hours Spent Per Week, and Course Duration as the independent variables, while Completion Status, Completion Percentage, Dropout Reason, and Satisfaction Score were treated as the dependent variables.

Story of Data: 
This project is an EdTech project that examines online learning behaviour among Freshers, Students, and Working Professionals.
The dataset shows how learners consume Technical and Non-Technical courses across different online learning platforms, including YouTube, Coursera, Udemy, Skillshare, and edX.

It provides information about the learners' experience level, course type, platform, hours spent per week, course duration, completion status, completion percentage, dropout reason, and satisfaction score.

The data provides an opportunity to understand the behaviours and circumstances associated with learners completing courses, remaining in progress, or dropping out. It also provides information that can help identify opportunities for improving learner satisfaction and platform performance.
The dataset is a public dataset obtained from Kaggle.

Conclusion:

The analysis of Online Learning Course Consumption provides useful insight into how learners interact with online education platforms and the factors associated with course completion, dropout, and satisfaction.

The findings show that course type, hours spent per week, platform, experience level, and course duration are associated with different learning outcomes. Non-Tech courses recorded a slightly higher completion share than Tech courses, while higher weekly study hours were associated with both increased completion and increased dropout. This indicates that learner engagement is important, but excessive demands may also create challenges for some learners.

The analysis also shows that dropout reasons vary across learner groups and platforms. Course difficulty and time constraints appeared repeatedly as important reasons associated with dropout. This highlights the need for EdTech providers and course creators to make courses more flexible, accessible, and manageable.

Platform performance also differed. Coursera recorded the highest completion share at 25%, while edX recorded the highest average satisfaction score of 3.2. On the other hand, Udemy recorded the lowest average satisfaction score at 2.8, while YouTube recorded the lowest completion share at 16%. These differences provide opportunities for platforms to study their learner experiences and identify areas for improvement.

Experience level also played an important role in the analysis. Working Professionals recorded the highest completion percentage at 36%, followed by Freshers at 34% and Students at 30%. However, the dropout reasons differed across these groups, suggesting that learners may require different types of support depending on their background and learning circumstances.

Overall, this project provides useful information for the EdTech industry. Platform providers can use these insights to improve course structures, reduce unnecessary difficulty, provide flexible learning options, improve learner support, and increase satisfaction. Course creators can also use the findings to design courses that are easier to manage and complete, while learners can use the insights to better understand the importance of consistent study habits and effective time management.

The ultimate goal should be to create online learning environments where learners are able to start courses, remain engaged, successfully complete them, and have a satisfying learning experience.
